# Supplementary Material S11. Systemic Osteogenic Set-point (SOS) theory — predictions and falsifying tests

This supplementary file operationalises the theoretical framework referenced in the main manuscript §4 (Biological context and predictions). It lists the six predictions (P1–P6) generated by the Systemic Osteogenic Set-point (SOS) theory and the seven falsifying tests (F1–F7), with the current paper's judgement on each. It is provided so that reviewers can verify the theory's claims against the reported evidence without relying on the main text alone.

## Core proposition

Common genetic variation sets a life-long, systemic bone-forming set-point, readable as areal BMD at any site. The set-point has **opposing-endpoint consequences**: at the whole-bone strength endpoint it is protective (against fracture); at the joint (osteochondral unit) endpoint it is pathogenic (raising OA risk through subchondral bone formation / osteophytes and TGF-β/WNT/BMP crosstalk). The name states the hypothesis, not an established mechanism: the present data index a BMD-raising set-point but do not resolve whether its OA-relevant action is bone formation (mass) or skeletal sizing (geometry), because the size proxy used here (height) captures neither bone width nor geometry [35]. Test F2 is the resolving experiment.

## Predictions and current judgement

| # | Prediction | Evidence in this study | Judgement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** Opposing endpoints | One BMD-raising profile protects against fracture yet raises OA risk | Positive control OR 1.40 (1.34–1.46) per SD lower BMD; BMD→knee-OA 5/5 positive (β +0.017 to +0.028) | Supported |
| **P2** Systemic, not local-mechanical | OA effect should not be largest at weight-bearing / joint-proximal sites; non-weight-bearing / axial sites should be ≥ femoral neck | Systemic clause: arms β +0.022 (p=1.7×10⁻⁶) — a non-weight-bearing site carries a highly significant effect, falsifying the local-mechanical rival; site homogeneity Q=6.92 (df=4), p=0.14 (F6). Ordering clause: arms +0.022 > femoral neck +0.017, one-sided p=0.18 | Systemic clause **formally supported**; ordering clause directionally consistent but **under-powered** (previously "not discriminating"; the homogeneity test excuses the site-correlation objection and is now executed) |
| **P3** Diffuse polygenic | No single locus should show BMD–OA colocalisation | 0/31 loci PP.H4≥0.8; strongest locus would need ~5×10⁷ participants (~42× present) for genome-wide significance | Direction consistent, **not discriminating** (a null is the predicted outcome under any diffuse mechanism) |
| **P4** Size is a separable partial component | Conditioning on height attenuates the OA effect but not the fracture effect | OA attenuation 9.5–33.2% (across the two tabulated baselines), a lower bound for total size; fracture positive control moved only −1.7% to −3.0% | Supported for the height-proxied share; the 67–91% height-independent residual is not identified as mass (F2 resolves) |
| **P5** Asymmetry is constitutional | Reverse (OA→BMD / OA→fracture) should be null, because a late, local disease cannot shift a life-long systemic set-point, and OA's bone effect is local | Reverse null (power 0.14–0.26) | Direction consistent + underpowered (both stated; power limitation not substituted for theory) |
| **P6** Site pathway dominance | Effect direction varies with joint dominant pathway: knee (systemic/metabolic) positive; hip (morphological/mechanical) reversed or attenuated | This study: knee positive. Literature: Zhou et al. [5] femoral-neck protective (hip); Hartley et al. [22] hip OR 1.28, knee OR 1.40 (both positive); Funck-Brentano et al. [36] all-OA OR 1.14 (1.06–1.22), significant at both knee and hip | **Weakened to a magnitude claim.** Hip evidence is internally contradictory (protective [5] vs harmful [22],[36]). Under the conjunctive falsification rule F1, Hartley et al. establishes positivity at the hip but not equality with the knee (log-OR 0.247 vs 0.336, 27% lower; overlapping CIs). Prior on F1 shifted against P6; F1 decision rule pre-registered (see below). |

## Falsifying tests (F6 and F7 executed here; F1–F5 prospective)

| # | Test | Design | Falsification condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **F1** Site pathway dominance | Repeat the ten-site design with **hip OA** as outcome | If hip OA effect is positive **and at least as large as** the knee effect → P6 fails (set-point would be whole-joint systemic, not knee-pathway-dominant) |
| **F2** Mass/size decomposition | Conditional MR with vBMD / hip geometry / bone-area GWAS as a second exposure (volumetric/cortical BMD is less size-confounded than areal BMD [37]; candidate second exposures include QCT volumetric BMD, bone area/width and hip-shape modes [35]) | If adding a true size phenotype attenuates the effect far beyond 33.2% → P4 residual is not "bone" |
| **F3** Mechanism localisation (bone vs cartilage side) | LDSC-SEG / MAGMA tissue enrichment of BMD instruments in osteoblast/bone vs chondrocyte/cartilage | If enrichment is in cartilage → mechanism direction overturned |
| **F4** Tissue-context colocalisation | eQTL colocalisation: bone-tissue eQTL↔OA vs cartilage eQTL↔OA (PP.H4 comparison) | If cartilage PP.H4 is systematically higher → "bone-driven" overturned |
| **F5** Phenotype stratification (cheapest, highest value; **pre-registered in the main text §4**) | Re-run MR stratifying OA outcome by osteophyte-predominant (hypertrophic) vs joint-space-narrowing-predominant | If the effect does not concentrate in the osteophyte-predominant subtype → the bone-forming-programme (SOS) hypothesis is overturned. Because osteophytes are the structural signature of the set-point, this is the discriminating test among the prospective set F1–F5 |
| **F6** Cross-site readout homogeneity (**executed here**; post-hoc, specified during peer-review revision with the decision rule fixed before execution and reported regardless of outcome) | Cochran-type Q on the five site-specific BMD→knee-OA IVW estimates, with the covariance induced by shared instruments and the shared OA outcome GWAS handled exactly: cov(A,B) = Σ_{i∈A∩B} β_e,i^A β_e,i^B / (W_A W_B se_o,i²); random-effects τ²; plus the two P2 clauses (systemic: non-weight-bearing arms effect ≠ 0; ordering: arms ≥ femoral neck) | If the five site estimates are heterogeneous (Q p<0.05) → the "one set-point readable at any site" claim fails and SOS retreats to site-specific statements. **Outcome: passed.** Q=6.92 (df=4), p=0.14; τ²=0 (DerSimonian–Laird); pooled β=+0.021 (95% CI +0.018 to +0.025); robust to the naive independence form (Q=3.56, p=0.47, anti-conservative because shared instruments induce correlations of 0.25–0.71) and to an equicorrelated conservative form (all pairwise ρ≥0.5: Q=7.65, p=0.105); 1 of 10 pairwise contrasts nominally significant (lumbar spine vs legs, p=0.022; chance-compatible at Bonferroni α=0.005). P2 systemic clause: arms β=+0.022 (p=1.7×10⁻⁶) → the local-mechanical rival (null at a non-weight-bearing site) is falsified; P2 ordering clause under-powered (arms − femoral neck +0.005, one-sided p=0.18) |
| **F7** Joint-locus exclusion (**executed here**; post-hoc, same labelling as F6) | Is the BMD→OA effect carried by joint-biology loci? (a) GDF5 (rs224333, chr20:34.0 Mb), the archetypal joint-development/OA locus, tested as a genome-wide-significant BMD instrument at each of the five sites; (b) leave-one-locus-out IVW excluding WNT16 (the strongest bone-formation locus) and SOX6 (chondrogenesis transcription factor) — WNT16, SOX6 and GDF5 are the BMD–OA shared-association candidate loci in the deposited locus screen | If the effect collapses when joint-biology loci are excluded → the estimate is carried by joint pleiotropy rather than by a bone-mediated set-point. **Outcome: passed.** GDF5 is not a genome-wide-significant BMD instrument at any site (absent from every harmonised instrument set and every deposited top-hits file); excluding WNT16 moves β by −0.4% to +32.3% (the estimate rises because WNT16's OA ratio is below the site average); excluding SOX6 moves β by +4.9% to +8.8%; no exclusion changes direction or significance, so no single annotated locus drives the effect |

**Honest boundaries (also in main-text Limitations):** SOS is inferred from the genetic architecture plus published biology, not directly measured; all BMD phenotypes are areal DXA and do not resolve subchondral micro-structure/turnover; the size partition is by height, a crude proxy that omits bone width and geometry [35], so neither the 9.5–33.2% nor the 67–91% share is a clean mass or size estimate; the multivariable analysis conditions on height and not on body-mass index, so a BMI-mediated component of the BMD→OA effect is not excluded and the height-independent share is a lower bound on the non-size component; the tests executed here (F6, F7) separate the set-point architecture from site-specific, local-mechanical and joint-locus rivals, but not from a generic systemic-pleiotropy rival in which any BMD-raising process also acts at the joint — that separation requires F2–F5 (volumetric decomposition, tissue enrichment, eQTL colocalisation, osteophyte-stratified outcome), none of which is available with the present data; the F6 homogeneity test pools five sites and one of its ten pairwise contrasts (lumbar spine vs legs, p=0.022) is nominally significant, chance-compatible at Bonferroni α=0.005 but not excluded, so we claim no detectable between-site variance rather than proven equality; Hartley et al. [22] additionally reported a reverse-direction estimate, which differs from this study's null reverse result; the divergence is attributable to design and scale (one-sample MR on the heel-ultrasound eBMD scale versus two-sample MR on the areal-BMD scale), not to a contradiction in the forward estimates, which agree across both studies; F3/F4 are specified as prospective tests requiring external resources (LDSC-SEG weights, eQTLGen); F5 is pre-registered in the main text §4 as the immediate next step and requires an OA GWAS with radiographic subtype information and has not been performed here. **Magnitude caveat:** the direction of the BMD→OA effect agrees with Hartley et al. [22] but the magnitude does not — our estimate is 12–20-fold smaller on the log-odds scale — and this gap is unresolved because the two exposures are different phenotypes on different scales (heel-ultrasound eBMD versus area-normalised DXA areal BMD) with different overlap structures (100% same-sample by construction versus ≤2.6% here). We claim directional, not magnitude, concordance. **Reproducibility:** the F6/F7 computations are archived in `code/124_verify_sos_discriminating.py` with outputs `code/output/sos_discriminating_verification.txt` and `code/output/sos_site_homogeneity.csv`; per-site IVW values recomputed from the deposited harmonised instrument sets reproduce Table 2 exactly.
