**Manuscript type:** Full length original research article
**Title:** "Genetically higher bone mineral density raises knee osteoarthritis risk yet protects against fracture: genetic correlation and bidirectional Mendelian randomisation across 13 skeletal phenotypes"

We submit the above full-length original research article for consideration as an Original Article in *Osteoarthritis and Cartilage*. The work has not been published previously, is not under consideration for publication elsewhere, and all authors have approved the manuscript and agree with its submission.

**Why this study fits the journal.** Osteoarthritis (OA) and bone mineral density (BMD) are the two most prevalent skeletal traits in ageing populations, yet whether higher BMD reflects shared aetiology with or causation of OA has been debated for five decades since Foss and Byers (1972). By jointly analysing 13 skeletal phenotypes — two OA definitions, ten regional BMD sites and any-fracture — with linkage-disequilibrium score regression and two-sample Mendelian randomisation, the study speaks directly to the osteochondral unit, a core theme of the journal, and to the clinical question of whether bone-targeted therapies carry an OA cost. We embed an internal positive control (BMD→fracture), dual FDR/Bonferroni correction, reverse MR, multivariable MR conditioning on adult height, and a STROBE-MR-compliant reporting workflow.

**Key findings.**
1. The 13-phenotype genetic-correlation matrix forms three blocks: BMD sites inter-correlate strongly (r_g 0.69–0.96), both OA definitions correlate positively with every BMD site (+0.12 to +0.25), and any-fracture correlates negatively with every BMD site (−0.45 to −0.60).
2. On pleiotropy-robust MR, genetically higher BMD raises knee-OA risk (β +0.017 to +0.028 per SD; p_IVW ≤ 6.4×10⁻⁴) while protecting against fracture (OR 1.40 per SD lower BMD; p = 3.7×10⁻⁵¹).
3. Multivariable MR shows 9.5–33.2% of the BMD→OA effect is attributable to the height-proxied size pathway (a lower bound for total size); the 67–91% height-independent residual is not resolved into bone mass versus skeletal geometry — quantifying the long-debated size-versus-mass confound of areal BMD.
4. Reverse MR (OA→BMD, OA→fracture) was null and underpowered (achieved power 0.14–0.26); no well-powered evidence supports reverse causation or an OA→fracture pathway.

**Mendelian randomisation compliance (OAC §1.1.5).** This study uses two-sample Mendelian randomisation; the following disclosures are provided as required by the journal's MR reporting policy.
- **Novelty relative to published MR.** Hartley et al. (*Int J Epidemiol* 2022) previously estimated the causal effect of composite BMD on OA independently of BMI. Our work extends that in four ways (ten regional BMD sites rather than one composite; conditioning on adult height rather than BMI; an internal BMD→fracture positive control; a full 13×13 LDSC matrix), and is, to our knowledge, the first MR to demonstrate opposing endpoint consequences — fracture-protective and knee-OA-raising — for a single BMD-raising genetic set-point.
- **Body-mass index.** BMD is genetically correlated with BMI, and BMI is itself a major knee-OA risk factor within the adiposity pathway. Our multivariable MR conditions on adult height (a skeletal-size proxy) rather than BMI, deliberately, to separate skeletal size from adiposity; we have not performed a BMI-conditioned sensitivity analysis because BMI summary statistics are not part of this deposit. The height-independent residual may therefore still contain a BMI-mediated component. This is stated in the Methods (§2.4) and Limitations (§6).
- **Instrument independence.** Instruments were selected at p<5×10⁻⁸ and clumped at r²<0.001 over 10 Mb against 1000 Genomes Phase 3 EUR; the only non-load-bearing cranial-head BMD site was excluded a priori because its instruments carry non-skeletal pleiotropy; directionality was confirmed by the Steiger test (Supplementary Table S13). Under standard MR assumptions the instruments affect the outcome only through the exposure.
- **Same underlying population / ancestry.** The BMD instruments are European-only, whereas the knee-OA outcome is 87.31% multi-ancestry (Supplementary Table S14) and LDSC uses a European-only reference. This mismatch can bias the estimates through cross-population LD differences and is disclosed in Limitations (§6); the forward estimates therefore generalise most directly to European-ancestry populations.
- **Translational value.** The opposing-endpoint pattern speaks directly to the osteochondral unit — a core theme of the journal — and quantifies a potential OA cost of bone-targeted (anti-resorptive or anabolic) therapy acting through a systemic bone-forming set-point, motivating joint-specific safety monitoring in trials and pharmacogenetic designs.
