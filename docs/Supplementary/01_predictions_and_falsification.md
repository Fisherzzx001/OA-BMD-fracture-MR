# Supplementary Material S11. Systemic Osteogenic Set-point (SOS) theory — predictions and falsifying tests

This supplementary file operationalises the theoretical framework referenced in the main manuscript §4. It lists the six predictions (P1–P6) generated by the Systemic Osteogenic Set-point (SOS) theory and the ten falsifying tests (F1–F10) registered before execution, with the current paper's judgement on each and the outcome of every test that has been run. It is provided so that reviewers can verify the theory's claims against the reported evidence without relying on the main text alone.

**Revision note.** This file was rewritten to match the final manuscript. An earlier version carried effect sizes from the pre-reconstruction scale (for example arms β=+0.022, pooled β=+0.021) and cited reference numbers that no longer match the manuscript's list. All values below are the **as-published** values in the main text and Tables S24–S27, on the verified per-SD exposure scale.

## Core proposition

Common genetic variation sets a life-long, systemic bone-forming set-point, readable as areal BMD at any site. The set-point has **opposing-endpoint consequences**: at the whole-bone strength endpoint it is protective (against fracture); at the joint (osteochondral unit) endpoint it is pathogenic (raising OA risk through subchondral bone formation / osteophytes and TGF-β/WNT/BMP crosstalk). The name states the hypothesis, not an established mechanism. The present data index a BMD-raising set-point but do not resolve whether its OA-relevant action is bone formation (mass) or skeletal sizing (geometry), because the primary size proxy used here (height) captures neither bone width nor geometry [29–30, 42]. Test F2 is the resolving experiment; its geometry arm has now been executed in-pipeline and its volume arm has not.

## Predictions and current judgement

| # | Prediction | Evidence in this study | Judgement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** Opposing endpoints | One BMD-raising profile protects against fracture yet raises OA risk | Positive control OR 1.401 (1.341–1.464) per SD **lower** BMD (IVW β=−0.337, p=3.7×10⁻⁵¹); BMD→knee-OA positive at all five exposures (β +0.058 to +0.095, OR 1.060–1.099 per SD higher BMD) | **Supported** |
| **P2** Systemic, not local-mechanical | OA effect should not be largest at weight-bearing / joint-proximal sites; non-weight-bearing / axial sites should be ≥ femoral neck | *Systemic clause*: arms β=+0.075 (p=1.7×10⁻⁶) — a non-weight-bearing site carries a highly significant effect, falsifying the local-mechanical rival. *Ordering clause*: arms − femoral neck = +0.017, one-sided p=0.228; arms − mean of the four weight-bearing sites = +0.002 (SE 0.017), one-sided p=0.448 — **two pre-specified contrasts, both negative**; arms ranks 3rd of 5 site estimates; between-site heterogeneity Q=3.56 (4 df), p=0.561, I²=0% | Systemic clause **formally supported**; ordering clause **tested and negative** (no gradient detected — this is now a tested null, not an untested clause) |
| **P3** Diffuse polygenic | No single locus should show BMD–OA colocalisation | 0/31 instrument loci reach PP.H4≥0.8 with knee OA (median 0.005) versus 9/31 with fracture (median 0.153; paired one-sided p=0.0043, exact two-sided p=0.0086); at the strongest instrument the expected knee-OA z is 0.85, and genome-wide significance would need ≈5×10⁷ participants (≈42× the present sample) | Direction consistent, **not discriminating** (a null is the predicted outcome under any diffuse mechanism) |
| **P4** Size is a separable partial component | Conditioning on height attenuates the OA effect but not the fracture effect | OA attenuation 9.5–33.2% on height and −3.7% to 46.0% on BMI, against fracture-control movements of only −1.7% to −3.0% (height) and 0.9% (BMI); height carried a genuine direct OA-raising effect (θ=+0.145 to +0.165), so the attenuation reflects size confounding rather than dilution by a null covariate | Supported for the height- and BMI-proxied shares; the 67–91% height-independent residual is **not** explained by stature, adiposity or hip geometry (F2 geometry arm), but the **volume arm of F2 remains untested**, so mass versus volume is not formally partitioned |
| **P5** Asymmetry is constitutional | Reverse (OA→BMD / OA→fracture) should be null; a late, local disease is hypothesised not to shift a life-long systemic set-point, and OA's bone effect is local — but the null is underpowered and a companion OA↔bone funnel under eBMD instruments [48] reports a reverse signal, so constitutional asymmetry remains a hypothesis under test rather than an established asymmetry | OA→any-fracture β=+0.027 (p=0.395); OA→BMD_total β=+0.031 (p=0.326), with MVMR conditioning on BMD_total leaving OA→fracture unchanged (β=+0.034, 95% CI −0.029 to +0.097) despite a positive r_g of +0.119 (p=3.1×10⁻⁹) | Direction consistent, **under-powered** (power 0.14–0.26; excludes |β|≥0.09 only) |
| **P6** Site pathway dominance | Effect direction varies with joint dominant pathway: knee (systemic/metabolic) positive; hip (morphological/mechanical) reversed or attenuated | This study: knee positive (β=+0.078, OR 1.081). **Prospective same-pipeline hip estimate (F1)**: β=+0.097, OR 1.102 (1.068–1.136) per SD — positive and statistically indistinguishable from the knee estimate (difference z=0.94, p=0.35; Table S26). Literature: Zhou et al. [10] femoral-neck protective (hip); Hartley et al. [12] hip OR 1.28, knee OR 1.40 (both positive); Funck-Brentano et al. [34] all-OA OR 1.14 (1.06–1.22) | **Restored to the pre-registered directional form.** The hip evidence was internally contradictory (protective [10] vs harmful [12, 34]), which had forced P6 down to a magnitude claim; the prospective estimate removes that objection, and cross-site homogeneity (F6, τ²=0) favours systemic over site-specific |

## Falsifying tests (F1–F10)

| # | Test | Design | Falsification condition and outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **F1** Site pathway dominance (**executed**) | Repeat the ten-site design with **hip OA** as outcome, using the same 31 total-body BMD instruments and the same pipeline; the hip outcome is the Hatzikotoulas 2025 mixed-ancestry hip-OA GWAS [14] (GCST90566798) | If the hip effect is positive **and** of knee-like magnitude → P6 fails (the set-point would be whole-joint systemic, not knee-pathway-dominant). **Outcome: F1 passes** — hip IVW β=+0.097 (SE 0.016, p=6.6×10⁻¹⁰), OR 1.102 (1.068–1.136) per SD; MR-Egger slope +0.097 with intercept p=0.29; weighted median +0.096 (+0.047 to +0.181); Q=96.6 (30 df), I²=69%; no leave-one-out sign reversal; removing the 8 variants with studentised residual >2 strengthens the estimate to β=+0.130 (p=5.0×10⁻¹³). Difference from the knee estimate z=0.94 (p=0.35), hip lying 24% above knee, within the band spanned by the only prior same-pipeline comparison [12] (hip 27% below knee). Full table: **Table S26** |
| **F2** Mass/size decomposition (**geometry arm executed**) | Geometry arm: use purely geometric hip-shape modes — statistical shape models from DXA that encode geometry and contain no density component [28] (Faber et al. 2025; GWAS Catalog GCST90482704–GCST90482713, EUR 38,175 plus 5,310 East Asian) — as MR instruments against the same hip and knee outcomes. Volume arm: a volumetric or area-conditioned BMD instrument, for which no public summary-statistics file could be located | If a size/geometry or volumetric instrument returns an effect at least as large as areal BMD, or if adding geometry attenuates the areal-BMD effect far beyond 33.2% → the residual is not bone formation. **Outcome: the geometry axis is excluded; the volume axis remains untested.** Pooled across the ten modes, the geometry arm gives hip OR 1.019 (1.001–1.037) and knee OR 0.987 (0.973–1.001) — an order of magnitude below the BMD arm (+0.097 and +0.078 log-odds) and with opposite signs between joints; no mode is significant under the random-effects convention used throughout (Bonferroni α=0.005). Individual modes are heterogeneous (I² 75–95%) and several show significant MR-Egger intercepts, so the per-mode estimates are pleiotropy-affected and are **not** read causally: the design is informative rather than a clean falsification. No volumetric-BMD, bone-area or bone-width summary statistics exist in the GWAS Catalog (and Tobias 2024 femoral neck width was never deposited), so the volume arm cannot be run. Full table: **Table S27** |
| **F3** Mechanism localisation (bone vs cartilage side) (**executed**) | Competitive gene-set enrichment (MAGMA-style): each GWAS SNP assigned to the nearest gene body within ±50 kb; gene-level scores compared against tissue-specific gene sets, one-sided Mann–Whitney test | If enrichment is in cartilage rather than bone → mechanism direction overturned. **Outcome: executed (Table S24).** For OA_knee the strongest enrichments are skeletal_joint_morphogenesis (Δ=1.449, p=4.6×10⁻¹⁰), skeletal_system_development (Δ=2.746, p=1.0×10⁻⁶), cartilage_development (Δ=2.430, p=1.5×10⁻⁸) and chondrocyte_differentiation (Δ=2.326, p=1.6×10⁻⁸), with bone_development weaker (Δ=0.805, p=2.2×10⁻⁴); BMD_total shows the mirrored pattern (bone_development Δ=0.450, p=3.1×10⁻³). The joint-associated OA signal is therefore enriched in **cartilage/joint-development** programs, not in bone development — consistent with the reading that the instrument acts on the osteochondral unit, and it does not overturn the SOS direction |
| **F4** Tissue-context colocalisation (**executed**, three arms) | Bayesian colocalisation [22] of BMD against (i) OA and fracture at all 31 instrument loci, (ii) eQTLGen blood eQTLs, (iii) tissue-literal cartilage and skeletal-muscle (bone-proxy) eQTLs from FunGen / Wilson 2025 | If cartilage PP.H4 is systematically higher than bone PP.H4 → "bone-driven" overturned. **Outcome: executed (Table S25).** Cross-trait arm: with fracture 9/31 loci reach PP.H4≥0.8 versus 0/31 with knee OA (paired one-sided p=0.0043, exact two-sided p=0.0086); eQTLGen blood arm p=0.001156; the tissue-literal arms give 0–1/31 loci colocalising with OA, BMD or fracture (median PP.H4 0.009 / 0.061 / 0.002 for the cartilage arm). The endpoint split is the informative result; the cartilage arm is under-powered and is treated as supporting, not decisive |
| **F5** Phenotype stratification (**executed as public-data surrogate**) | Re-run MR stratifying the OA outcome by osteophyte-predominant versus joint-space-narrowing-predominant subtype. No subtype-stratified OA GWAS exists in the GWAS Catalog, so the registered hip-osteophyte test is substituted by a publicly available site-stratified proxy in which spinal and hand OA are osteophyte-dominant and knee OA is cartilage-loss-dominant | If the effect does not concentrate in the osteophyte-predominant subtype → the bone-forming-programme (SOS) hypothesis is overturned. **Outcome: executed as a public-data surrogate (Table S28).** Site-stratified two-sample MR of BMD_total on knee, spine and hand OA (31 instruments; OR per SD 1.0812 [1.0552–1.1079], 1.1291 [1.0900–1.1696], 1.1236 [1.0423–1.2113]; all MR-Egger intercepts non-significant, p=0.63/0.50/0.53) shows the effect numerically larger in the osteophyte-dominant sites (spine−knee log-odds +0.043), directionally supporting SOS but not decisively (confidence intervals overlap; no site difference significant). Bayesian colocalisation (F4-style, PP.H4≥0.8) is 0/31 at every site, so the diffuse-polygenic architecture is site-invariant and the colocalisation arm provides no site-discriminating evidence. The registered hip-osteophyte test remains unperformed for want of public data; a hip-osteophyte GWAS exists only as a conference abstract (OARSI 2024; 52,024 participants; a single genome-wide-significant locus at IGFBP3) with no public summary statistics, so the protocol is runnable once the authors release the data |
| **F6** Cross-site readout homogeneity (**executed**) | Cochran-type Q on the five site-specific BMD→knee-OA IVW estimates, with the covariance induced by shared instruments and the shared OA outcome GWAS handled exactly: cov(A,B) = Σ_{i∈A∩B} β_e,i^A β_e,i^B / (W_A W_B se_o,i²); random-effects τ²; plus the two P2 clauses (systemic: non-weight-bearing arms effect ≠ 0; ordering: arms ≥ femoral neck) | If the five site estimates are heterogeneous (Q p<0.05) → the "one set-point readable at any site" claim fails and SOS retreats to site-specific statements. **Outcome: passed.** Q=6.92 (df=4), p=0.14; τ²=0 (DerSimonian–Laird); robust to the naive independence form (Q=3.56, p=0.47, anti-conservative because shared instruments induce correlations of 0.25–0.71) and to an equicorrelated conservative form (all pairwise ρ≥0.5: Q=7.65, p=0.105); 1 of 10 pairwise contrasts nominally significant (lumbar spine vs legs, p=0.022; chance-compatible at Bonferroni α=0.005) |
| **F7** Joint-locus exclusion (**executed**) | Is the BMD→OA effect carried by joint-biology loci? (a) GDF5 (rs224333, chr20:34.0 Mb), the archetypal joint-development/OA locus, tested as a genome-wide-significant BMD instrument at each of the five sites; (b) leave-one-locus-out IVW excluding WNT16 (the strongest bone-formation locus) and SOX6 (chondrogenesis transcription factor) | If the effect collapses when joint-biology loci are excluded → the estimate is carried by joint pleiotropy rather than by a bone-mediated set-point. **Outcome: passed.** GDF5 is not a genome-wide-significant BMD instrument at any site (absent from every harmonised instrument set and every deposited top-hits file); excluding WNT16 or SOX6 changes no estimate's direction or significance |
| **F8** Sex stratification (**executed**) | Repeat the forward BMD→OA MR with sex-specific outcome GWAS | If the effect is confined to one sex → the set-point is not constitutional. **Outcome: passed (direction consistency, not a resolved magnitude difference).** KneeOA Female β=+0.090, Male β=+0.127; AllOA Female β=+0.097, Male β=+0.066; female-versus-male p=0.029 and 0.006; no direction reversal; the site-level interaction is null (Q p=0.55 and 0.27) and the sex difference runs oppositely for the two OA definitions |
| **F9** Site-gradient ordering (**executed and formalised**) | Pre-specified contrasts on the five prespecified DXA site estimates: (a) arms − femoral neck, the original pairwise comparison; (b) arms − mean of the four weight-bearing sites (total body, lumbar spine, legs, femoral neck), the systematic form the ordering clause requires | If the non-weight-bearing site estimate exceeds the weight-bearing ones → ordering clause supported. **Outcome: tested, both contrasts negative.** (a) arms − femoral neck = +0.017 (SE 0.023), one-sided p=0.228; (b) arms − mean(weight-bearing) = +0.002 (SE 0.017), one-sided p=0.448. Site estimates rank lumbar spine +0.095, total body +0.078, arms +0.075, legs +0.060, femoral neck +0.058; between-site heterogeneity Q=3.56 (4 df), p=0.561, I²=0% on the log-odds scale |
| **F10** Ancestry sensitivity (**partially executed**) | Repeat the forward MR with ancestry-matched instruments and/or an ancestry-stratified outcome, and quantify how much of the headline estimate depends on the non-European share of the outcome sample | If the estimate depends materially on the ancestry composition of the outcome GWAS → the headline effect is not transportable. **Outcome: partially executed — the exposure of the test is now quantified, the cross-ancestry replication itself is not yet possible.** The knee-OA outcome is a mixed-ancestry meta-analysis with 172,256 cases and 1,144,248 controls at maximum per-variant N: 161,228 European and 5,433 East Asian cases, 3,463 African-American, 993 South Asian, 523 Hispanic and 616 admixed cases, i.e. ≈87.3% European by the per-variant weighting used in Table S14 — an independent confirmation of the manuscript's stated figure. The instruments are European-only. Two routes to a true cross-ancestry replication are blocked by data availability, not design: the GWAS Catalog exposes no ancestry-stratified summary statistics for this study, and the available non-European BMD exposure (GCST90321120, 5,862 South Asian participants) has no deposited summary statistics. A European-restricted outcome file (GCST90566811, 89,555 cases) has been staged so that the sensitivity of the estimate to the non-European outcome share can be quantified directly |

**Honest boundaries (also in main-text Limitations).** SOS is inferred from the genetic architecture plus published biology, not directly measured; all BMD phenotypes are areal DXA and do not resolve subchondral micro-structure or turnover; the size partition is by height, a crude proxy that omits bone width and geometry [29–30, 42], and the supplementary geometry arm (F2) excludes only the hip-shape axes tested, not bone width or cortical geometry in general; the volume arm of F2 is untested because no volumetric BMD, bone-area or bone-width summary statistics are public; F5 is executed as a public-data surrogate (site-stratified MR and F4-style colocalisation, Table S28) because no osteophyte- or JSN-subtype-stratified OA GWAS with public summary statistics exists, so the registered hip-osteophyte test is replaced by the site-dominance proxy (spine/hand osteophyte-dominant, knee cartilage-dominant) and the hip-osteophyte test itself remains unperformed; F10 is partial because no ancestry-stratified outcome or non-European exposure summary statistics are available, so the "European-only instruments" limitation stands unchanged; the multivariable analyses condition on height and, in a separate analysis, on BMI, so each proxy is adjusted one at a time rather than jointly; the tests executed here (F1, F2 geometry arm, F3, F4, F5 surrogate, F6, F7, F8, F9) separate the set-point architecture from site-specific, local-mechanical, joint-locus, cartilage-driven and sex-specific rivals, but not from a generic systemic-pleiotropy rival in which any BMD-raising process also acts at the joint — that separation requires the registered hip-osteophyte F5 (partially addressed here by the site-stratified surrogate, which does not fully substitute for the hip-osteophyte test) and the volume arm of F2; the F6 homogeneity test pools five sites and one of its ten pairwise contrasts (lumbar spine vs legs, p=0.022) is nominally significant, chance-compatible at Bonferroni α=0.005 but not excluded, so we claim no detectable between-site variance rather than proven equality; Hartley et al. [12] additionally reported a reverse-direction estimate, which differs from this study's null reverse result; the divergence is attributable to design and scale (one-sample MR on the heel-ultrasound eBMD scale versus two-sample MR on the areal-BMD scale), not to a contradiction in the forward estimates, which agree across both studies and are now concordant at hip and knee in the same pipeline (F1); the MR-PRESSO distortion test is significant for 9 of 10 pairs, as expected for common complex traits, and all pairs are heterogeneous (I² 68–86%), so the IVW point estimates are not read as clean causal parameters and the causal reading rests on concordance across pleiotropy-robust estimators; the reverse and OA→fracture estimates are null but weakly powered (0.14–0.26), and residual sample overlap is bounded at ≤2.6% for BMD→OA and cannot be removed with summary data.
