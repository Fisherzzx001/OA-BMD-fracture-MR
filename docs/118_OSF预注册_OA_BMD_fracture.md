## 1. Study information

- **Title.** Genetically higher bone mineral density raises knee osteoarthritis risk yet protects against fracture: genetic correlation and bidirectional Mendelian randomisation across 13 skeletal phenotypes.
- **Study type.** Secondary analysis of published GWAS summary statistics (no new samples).
- **Design.** Triangulated genetic epidemiology: (i) LDSC genetic correlation across 78 phenotype pairs; (ii) two-sample Mendelian randomisation for 10 MR pairs, bidirectional (forward BMD→OA/fracture, reverse OA→BMD/fracture); (iii) multivariable MR conditioning on height; (iv) MRlap sample-overlap correction; (v) colocalisation of BMD with knee OA.

## 2. Research question

1. Does a higher BMD-raising genetic set-point **share genetic architecture** with osteoarthritis (OA) and with fracture?
2. Does it exert **opposing causal effects** at the joint (OA) versus the whole-bone strength (fracture) endpoints?
3. Is the joint (OA) effect **mediated by skeletal size, bone mass, or neither** — i.e. can the height-independent residual be resolved?

## 3. Background & rationale

The epidemiological association between higher areal BMD and OA has been debated since Foss and Byers reported raised femoral-head BMD in osteoarthritic hips in 1972 [11]; osteoporosis and OA were long regarded as opposing conditions [12]. Prior genetic work gave only partial answers: Hackinger et al. reported r_g = +0.18 between hip/knee OA and lumbar-spine BMD (p = 0.022) [4]; Zhou et al. reported r_g = +0.13 by LDSC and +0.17 by HDL between hip OA and femoral-neck BMD [5]; a 2025 MR of BMD against OA progression reported the same site-by-joint dependence [31]. What remained unsettled was the **causal direction** and whether the joint effect is a **size or mass** phenomenon. We address this with a single calibrated pipeline spanning 13 skeletal phenotypes, an internal BMD→fracture positive control, and a height-conditioning decomposition. The Systemic Osteogenic Set-point (SOS) is used as a **heuristic** for organising results, not a fitted model.

## 4. Hypotheses

- **H1.** BMD and OA are genetically correlated (r_g > 0).
- **H2.** Genetically proxied higher BMD raises knee-OA risk (forward MR β > 0).
- **H3.** Genetically proxied higher BMD protects against fracture (positive control; forward MR β < 0 for fracture, OR > 1 per SD lower BMD).
- **H4.** OA does not cause BMD or fracture (reverse MR null).
- **H5.** Part of the BMD→OA effect is mediated by skeletal size (height-conditioning attenuates it) but a substantial height-independent residual remains.

## 7b. Revision-phase analyses (executed after preregistration; reported transparently)

These analyses were not part of the preregistered falsification battery. They were executed during revision to close limitations stated in the manuscript itself, and are recorded here for completeness.

- **BMI-conditioned MVMR (closes the manuscript's "BMI-mediated component not excluded" limitation).** Source: GIANT–UK Biobank BMI meta-analysis (Pulit et al. 2019, *Hum Mol Genet* 28(1):166–174, PMID 30239722; summary statistics from Zenodo 1251813, N≈806,800, md5-verified in the project pipeline). Design mirrors the height MVMR (Table S9): same estimators, Sanderson–Smith conditional-F gate, primary n_bmi=100 (61–63 instruments survived harmonisation), grid n_bmi ∈ {100,300,800}. Decision rule fixed before execution: if θ_BMD lost significance or reversed under BMI conditioning, the height-independent residual would be read as BMI-mediated. Result: θ_BMD stayed positive and fixed-effect-significant at all five sites (total-body +0.0203, p=2.6×10⁻⁸; attenuation 11.9–46% vs the Table 2 baseline, below the height attenuation at four of five sites); the BMD→fracture positive control moved 0.9%; θ_BMI→fracture was null (p=0.61) while θ_BMI→knee-OA was strong (β≈+0.20). The residual is therefore not BMI-mediated. Height and BMI were conditioned separately, not jointly. Code: `code/163_mvmr_bmi.py`; outputs `code/output/genetics/mvmr_bmi_main.csv` / `mvmr_bmi_instruments.csv`; table `docs/Supplementary/Table_S16_mvmr_bmi.{md,csv}`; manuscript §4/§99, abstract, Methods and Data availability updated.

## 7. Analysis plan (as performed / to be performed)

- **Exposures.** Ten regional BMD sites (Qian et al. 2025, N ≈ 1.873 each) [2]; total-body BMD for colocalisation regions.
- **Outcomes.** Radiographic knee OA (OA_knee; GO Consortium 2025, N ≈ 1.21M) [1]; self-reported OA (OA_selfrep; IEU `ukb-b-14486`, N = 462,933) [3]; any-fracture (GEFOS 2018, N = 264,973; 37,857 cases) [10].
- **Instruments.** Strict clumping r² < 0.001 within 10 Mb; European 1000G Phase 3 LD reference; harmonisation by allele frequency.
- **MR methods.** IVW (fixed + random effects), MR-Egger (intercept pleiotropy test), weighted median (bootstrap CI), MR-PRESSO (outlier removal), MRlap (sample-overlap correction).
- **Multivariable MR.** BMD + height (Table S9; primary n_h = 100 / EUR; MVMR-set baseline) and BMD + BMI (Table S16; primary n_bmi = 100; GIANT–UK Biobank BMI meta-analysis, Pulit et al. 2019 [38]) — conditioned separately, not jointly.
- **Colocalisation.** Priors p1 = p2 = 1×10⁻⁴, p12 = 5×10⁻⁶; PP.H4 ≥ 0.8 threshold retained.
- **Sensitivity.** Steiger directionality test, leave-one-out, leave-one-locus-out.
- **Prospective extensions (F1–F5).** documented above; execution contingent on external GWAS / weights (hip-OA, QCT/geometry, LDSC-SEG, eQTLGen, subtype OA).

## 8. Statistical thresholds

- Genetic correlation / MR: false-discovery-rate (FDR) < 0.05, with Bonferroni reported for pairwise contrasts.
- Colocalisation: PP.H4 ≥ 0.8 for a shared causal variant; PP.H3 ≥ 0.8 for distinct variants.
- Heterogeneity: Cochran Q with random-effects τ²; nominal p reported with Bonferroni α = 0.005 for the ten pairwise contrasts.
